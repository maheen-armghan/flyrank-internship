# Prioritizing Content Refresh Decisions with Observable Search Signals

## Abstract
FlyRank helps clients manage large, growing content libraries — but as those libraries scale, manually reviewing every page for refresh, expansion, or pruning becomes impossible. Clients need a way to answer one practical question: out of thousands of pages, which ones deserve a reviewer's limited attention first? This paper treats that question as a case study in content-operations decision support. Using a 30,000-page anonymized slice of FlyRank's real search-performance data, I built and validated a content-refresh prioritization pipeline — from problem framing through a data contract, a signal audit, a baseline rule, a trained model comparison, and a final action playbook. The central finding was not that a model beat a hand-written rule (the common expectation): under an honest, leakage-free, client-grouped evaluation, a simple two-signal baseline rule (declining trend + real traffic demand) achieved Precision@50 = 0.64, outperforming logistic regression, decision tree, and random forest models (0.58-0.60). This result is reported honestly as decision-support evidence for FlyRank's content review workflow, not as a universal or automated claim.

## Introduction / Problem Statement
FlyRank's product surfaces content health signals so clients can decide where to invest limited content-review capacity — but that capacity is always finite, while content libraries keep growing. This is the real, concrete case study behind this project: a FlyRank client with thousands of pages and a reviewer who can only manually check a small fraction of them each week. Which pages should that reviewer look at first? This paper explores one lane of that problem — Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring — ranking pages by observed decline and demand signals rather than requiring a client to scan their full inventory. The work documents the full pipeline built to answer this: a research question grounded in that real content-operations decision, a transparent data contract, a signal audit testing two FlyRank product-relevant beliefs (staleness-based refresh triggers, and CTR-vs-position expectations), a validated baseline, a model comparison under honest evaluation, and a final ranked action playbook — including an explicit, honest account of where the trained models underperformed a simpler rule, and what that means for how FlyRank might prioritize future refinement.

## Data
This project uses the small anonymized starter dataset shipped with the FlyRank ML Internship starter repo (`content_refresh_anonymized.csv`, ~30,000 pages), plus schema exploration of the larger `FlyRank/internship-warehouse` release on Hugging Face (build id `flyrank_pseudonymized_warehouse_release_v20260703`; fact table spanning 2025-01-27 to 2026-06-30). No client names, domains, URLs, or raw queries are included in either release. All identifiers are pseudonymized hashes. Rows were filtered to `impressions_90d > 0` and `content_age_days >= 90`, and deduplicated by `content_id`. FlyRank's own product decision outputs (`health_score`, `priority_score`, `action_type`) are deliberately excluded from the dataset and were never used as features, to avoid circular results.

## Methodology
**Task type:** binary classification (declining vs. not), used downstream to produce a ranking.
**Label/proxy:** `trend_direction == "down"`, a current-window proxy, not a validated future outcome.
**Features:** `impressions_90d`, `avg_position`, `content_age_days`, `word_count`, `days_since_last_update`, `ctr`, `sessions_90d`, and related observable signals, all confirmed knowable before any refresh decision.
**Baseline:** a two-condition rule (declining AND `impressions_90d >= 100`), weighted by impression volume.
**Validation design:** client-grouped holdout split, preventing pages from the same client appearing in both
